Phase 3 · The Reasoning Paradox

Smarter models, worse grounding

Vectara's 2026 hallucination benchmark used harder, longer documents — law, medicine, finance. GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Grok-4, and Gemini-3-Pro all crossed 10% hallucination. A lightweight non-reasoning model (Gemini Flash-Lite) stayed at 3.3%.

"Reasoning models spend extra compute 'thinking through' an answer. During that thinking, they infer, connect dots, and generate insight beyond what's in the source material. That's great for math. It's hallucination when you need the model to just stick to the facts."

"We're building models that are better at sounding confident than at being right. In production engineering, confident-and-wrong is the most expensive failure mode there is."

Source: Vectara Hallucination Leaderboard, 2026 update

Phase 4 · 5 Gaps AI Can't Bridge

Not a bug. An architectural limitation.

So far this is "what AI gets wrong." Now let's talk about what it fundamentally can't do.

GAP–ROUTER
NO GROUND TRUTH
01

System Design

SWE-bench Verified has reached roughly 95–97% for leading systems and is now saturated; SWE-bench Pro is the harder benchmark to reference here — on isolated bugs with a known correct patch. System design has no ground truth to benchmark against at all.

02

Debugging Legacy Code

METR RCT: 16 experienced devs, 5-year-old codebases. With AI tools, they were 19% slower in the 2025 RCT; later ~18% faster data was unreliable — not the 24% faster they'd predicted, and not the 20% faster they still believed afterward.

03

Cross-Team Coordination

AI can draft the Slack message. It can't decide whether to send it, or sense the other team lead is already burnt out this sprint.

04

Business Context & Intent

AI implements the ticket exactly as written. It doesn't know "sort by price" is a compliance requirement, or that this bug touches a $2M/day payment flow.

05

Handling Ambiguity

"Make it faster" needs a senior engineer's judgment call with 60% information. AI needs a defined objective function. Ambiguity resolution is most of real engineering work.

Phase 5 · The Cost Reality

Token cost isn't the real cost

Agentic loop overhead

Plan → generate → test → fix → retest burns 10–50× the tokens of a one-shot answer on legacy code needing large context.

Verification cost

METR's core finding, as economics: AI shifts engineer time from writing into reviewing/correcting — and reviewing someone else's code is cognitively more expensive than writing your own.

Security debt

AI-linked CVEs rose from 6 → 35 per month in one Georgia Tech study; Faros AI reported bugs +54% and incidents >3×. These are correlational signals, not causal proof. means building a dedicated AI-code security review pipeline that didn't exist two years ago.

The real ROI question isn't "can AI write code faster" — it's "does AI reduce total cost from first commit to production-stable." Current best evidence says: not yet, not for experienced devs on mature systems.

AGI–ROUTER

That combination is close to a working definition of AGI itself. Until it exists, the ~40/60 split is a rule of thumb, not a study result: AI is strongest on mechanical work, while judgment-heavy engineering remains harder to automate end-to-end.

"AI is the most powerful autocomplete ever built. It'll make a great engineer 10× more productive on the mechanical parts.

It will not replace the engineer who understands architecture, business context, team dynamics, and has the judgment to ship with incomplete information."

"The biggest advantage may go to engineers who use AI to reduce mechanical work and reinvest the saved time in architecture, verification, product context, and higher-leverage decisions."

What to remember

01

AI accelerates typing, not judgment — and judgment is most of the job.

02

METR’s 2025 RCT found experienced developers took 19% longer with AI on the tested tasks; later late-2025 data suggested ~18% faster, but METR said that later dataset was unreliable. The evidence is mixed, not a universal speedup or slowdown.

03

Full replacement needs AGI-level reasoning + memory + social modeling — not just a better autocomplete.
